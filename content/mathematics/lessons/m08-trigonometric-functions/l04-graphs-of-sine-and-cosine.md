# Graphs of Sine and Cosine

Imagine you are standing on a beach, watching the tide rise and fall. The water level does not change in a straight line; instead, it pulses in a smooth, rhythmic cycle. In our last lesson, we explored the unit circle, where we defined sine and cosine based on the coordinates of points as they travel around a circle of radius one. When we map those circular values onto a coordinate plane, we get the classic wave patterns that describe this rhythmic motion.

The sine function is an odd function, meaning its graph is symmetric about the origin. It begins at zero when the input is zero, climbs to a peak of one at pi over two, comes back down through zero at pi, reaches a valley of negative one at three pi over two, and returns to zero at two times pi radians. The cosine function is an even function, meaning it is symmetric about the vertical axis. It begins at one when the input is zero. In fact, the cosine graph is simply the sine graph shifted to the left by pi over two radians. Both functions repeat their values every two times pi radians, which we call the period, and both are bounded between negative one and one.

To understand how these waves transform, we use the general form where the vertical value equals the coefficient, let us call it m, times the sine of the quantity k times x minus h, plus a vertical shift, d. The absolute value of m is the amplitude. Multiplying the output by m scales the wave vertically because every height value is stretched by that factor. If m is three, the wave reaches up to three and down to negative three. Adding d shifts the entire wave vertically, moving the midline to the value d.

The term k inside the sine function affects the period. The period is two times pi divided by the absolute value of k. If we multiply the input x by two, the wave completes its cycle twice as fast, shrinking the period to pi. This happens because the input reaches the same cycle completion point at half the distance. Finally, the value h divided by k represents the phase shift, which pushes the wave left or right.

Let us look at a specific example. Suppose we have the function where the vertical value is two times the sine of the quantity three times x. The amplitude is two, so the wave stretches between negative two and two. The period is two times pi divided by three. If we add one to the end of that expression, we shift the entire wave up by one unit, so the midline is now at one, and the wave oscillates between negative one and three.

These waves are not just abstract lines; they are the language of the natural world. Daylight hours follow a periodic pattern throughout the year. By adjusting the midline, amplitude, and period, mathematicians create models that predict sunlight hours for any location.

While sine and cosine create rolling waves, the tangent function behaves differently. Remember that tangent is the ratio of sine to cosine. Because the cosine value equals zero at odd multiples of pi over two, such as pi over two or three times pi over two, the tangent function is undefined at those points. On a graph, this creates vertical asymptotes where the curve shoots toward infinity or negative infinity. Unlike sine and cosine, which are bounded, the tangent function grows without limit. It has a period of pi, repeating its S-shaped curve between every pair of consecutive asymptotes.

Understanding these waves gives us the power to map everything from sound frequencies to the movement of light. When we know how to shift, stretch, and compress these curves, we can describe complex periodic behaviors with precise mathematical expressions.

## Key ideas

- The sine function is odd and the cosine function is even, both having a period of two times pi and a range from negative one to one.
- The amplitude is the absolute value of the leading coefficient, while the period is two times pi divided by the absolute value of the coefficient of the input.
- The vertical shift moves the midline of the wave, and the phase shift is determined by dividing the horizontal displacement constant by the coefficient of the input.
- The tangent function has a period of pi and vertical asymptotes occurring where the cosine of the input equals zero.
